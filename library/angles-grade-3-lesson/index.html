Angles

Read the diagram. Pick the fact. Do the maths. One small step at a time.

The six facts you need

Every angle question uses one of these. You don't need to remember anything else.

1 · Straight lineAngles on a straight line add to 180°
2 · PointAngles at a point add to 360°
3 · Vertically oppositeWhen two lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal
4 · TriangleThe three angles in a triangle add to 180°
5 · QuadrilateralThe four angles in a four-sided shape add to 360°
6 · Parallel linesF-shape (corresponding) = equal · Z-shape (alternate) = equal · C-shape (co-interior) = add to 180°

Worked example 1 — angles on a straight line

Three angles sit on a straight line: 56°, x, and 72°. Find x.

  1. Read it. Three angles meet on one straight line. We need to find the middle one, x.
  2. The fact. Angles on a straight line add to 180°.
  3. The numbers. We know 56° and 72°. We do not know x.
  4. Add the known angles. 56 + 72 = 128.
  5. Take that from 180. 180 − 128 = 52.
Answer: x = 52°
Watch outSome pupils add all three: 56 + x + 72 = 360. That is wrong. A point is 360°. A straight line is 180°.

Worked example 2 — angles at a point

Four angles meet at a point: 120°, 95°, 78°, and x. Find x.

  1. Read it. Four rays leave the same dot. The whole way round is one full turn.
  2. The fact. Angles at a point add to 360°.
  3. The numbers. We know 120°, 95° and 78°. We do not know x.
  4. Add the known angles. 120 + 95 + 78 = 293.
  5. Take that from 360. 360 − 293 = 67.
Answer: x = 67°
Watch outA point goes all the way round (360°). A straight line only goes half-way round (180°). Use 360°, not 180°.

Worked example 3 — vertically opposite angles

Two straight lines cross. One angle is 64°. Find the angle directly opposite, x.

  1. Read it. Two lines cross at one dot. They make four angles. We need the one opposite the 64°.
  2. The fact. When two lines cross, opposite angles are equal.
  3. Look at the matching ticks. The little tick lines show which two angles are a matching pair.
  4. So they are the same number. x is the same as 64°.
Answer: x = 64°
Watch outPupils sometimes pick the angle next to 64° instead of the one opposite. Next-to angles are NOT equal — they add to 180°. The opposite one (with the matching tick) is the equal one.

Worked example 4 — angles in a triangle

A triangle has two angles of 47° and 68°. Find the third angle, x.

  1. Read it. A triangle. Two angles known. One missing.
  2. The fact. The three angles in a triangle add to 180°.
  3. The numbers. We know 47° and 68°. We do not know x.
  4. Add the known angles. 47 + 68 = 115.
  5. Take that from 180. 180 − 115 = 65.
Answer: x = 65°
Watch outTriangles add to 180°. Squares and rectangles add to 360°. Do not mix them up.

Worked example 5 — angles in a quadrilateral

A four-sided shape has angles 85°, 95°, 110° and x. Find x.

  1. Read it. A shape with four corners. Three angles known. One missing.
  2. The fact. The four angles in a four-sided shape add to 360°.
  3. The numbers. We know 85°, 95° and 110°. We do not know x.
  4. Add the known angles. 85 + 95 + 110 = 290.
  5. Take that from 360. 360 − 290 = 70.
Answer: x = 70°
Watch outIt is easy to forget the total is 360° for a four-sided shape, not 180°. A triangle has 3 sides → 180°. A four-sided shape has 4 sides → 360°.

Worked example 6 — parallel lines — corresponding (F-shape)

Two parallel lines are crossed by another line. One angle is 58°. Find x, the matching (corresponding) angle.

  1. Read it. Two parallel lines. A line crosses both of them.
  2. Spot the shape. The 58° and x make an F-shape (look for the F lying down). That means they are corresponding angles.
  3. The fact. Corresponding angles are equal.
  4. The answer is the same number. x is the same as 58°.
Answer: x = 58°
Watch outDifferent shapes give different rules: F = equal · Z = equal · C = add to 180°. Always check the shape first.

Worked example 7 — parallel lines — alternate (Z-shape)

Two parallel lines are crossed by another line. One angle is 64°. Find the marked angle, y.

  1. Read it. Two parallel lines. A line crosses both of them.
  2. Spot the shape. The 64° and y make a Z-shape. That means they are alternate angles.
  3. The fact. Alternate angles are equal.
  4. The answer is the same number. y is the same as 64°.
Answer: y = 64°
Watch outThe Z-shape needs both angles to be INSIDE the parallel lines, one on each side of the crossing line. If they are not inside, it is not a Z.

Worked example 8 — parallel lines — co-interior (C-shape)

Two parallel lines are crossed by another line. One angle is 72°. Find x, the co-interior angle (on the same side, both inside the parallel lines).

  1. Read it. Two parallel lines. A line crosses both of them. Two angles sit on the same side of the crossing line, both between the parallels.
  2. Spot the shape. The 72° and x make a C-shape. That means they are co-interior angles.
  3. The fact. Co-interior angles add to 180° (they are NOT equal).
  4. Take the known angle from 180. 180 − 72 = 108.
Answer: x = 108°
Watch outF and Z give EQUAL angles. C is the odd one out — co-interior angles ADD to 180°. The C-shape is the "add up" one.
