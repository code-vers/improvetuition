Estimation

Round each number to make it easier. Then compute. The answer will be close — that's the point.

The six facts you need

Every estimation question uses one of these. You don't need to remember anything else.

1 · What estimation isFinding an answer that is CLOSE, not exact. Fast and rough.
2 · How to estimateRound each number to make it easier — then do the calculation.
3 · Round to 1 sig figKeep the first digit; the rest become zeros. So 274 → 300 · 4.7 → 5 · 0.032 → 0.03.
4 · Add / SubtractRound each number; then add or subtract.
5 · Multiply / DivideRound each number so they multiply or divide easily (like 80 × 40 or 600 ÷ 30).
6 · The ≈ symbol≈ means "approximately equal to". Use it when writing estimates.

Worked example 1 - round to 1 sig fig

Round 274 to 1 significant figure.

  1. Read it. Round 274 to 1 sig fig.
  2. The fact. Keep the first digit; the rest become zeros. Look at the next digit for the up-or-down rule.
  3. First digit of 274: 2 (in the hundreds).
  4. Next digit: 7. 7 is 5 or more, so round UP.
  5. 2 hundreds → 3 hundreds → 300.
Answer: 300
Watch out1 sig fig = round to the BIGGEST column of the number.

Worked example 2 - round a decimal to 1 sig fig

Round 4.7 to 1 significant figure.

  1. Read it. Round 4.7 to 1 sig fig.
  2. The fact. For a number between 1 and 10, 1 sig fig = nearest whole number.
  3. 4.7. First digit: 4 (ones).
  4. Next digit: 7. Round UP.
  5. 4 → 5.
Answer: 5
Watch outFor 4.7 the biggest column is ones. Round to a whole number.

Worked example 3 - estimate addition

Estimate 274 + 189 by rounding each to 1 sig fig.

  1. Read it. Estimate 274 + 189.
  2. The fact. Round each to 1 sig fig, then add.
  3. 274 → 300.
  4. 189 → 200.
  5. 300 + 200 = 500.
Answer: ≈ 500
Watch outThe estimate is NOT the exact answer. It just tells you the right ballpark.

Worked example 4 - estimate subtraction

Estimate 856 − 439 by rounding each to 1 sig fig.

  1. Read it. Estimate 856 − 439.
  2. The fact. Round each, then subtract.
  3. 856 → 900.
  4. 439 → 400.
  5. 900 − 400 = 500.
Answer: ≈ 500
Watch outEstimates can be over or under the exact answer. They're just close.

Worked example 5 - estimate multiplication

Estimate 82 × 39 by rounding each to 1 sig fig.

  1. Read it. Estimate 82 × 39.
  2. The fact. Round each, then multiply.
  3. 82 → 80.
  4. 39 → 40.
  5. 80 × 40 = 3200.
Answer: ≈ 3200
Watch outRounded numbers with lots of zeros are easy to multiply. That's the point.

Worked example 6 - estimate division

Estimate 621 ÷ 29 by rounding each to 1 sig fig.

  1. Read it. Estimate 621 ÷ 29.
  2. The fact. Round each so they divide easily, then divide.
  3. 621 → 600.
  4. 29 → 30.
  5. 600 ÷ 30 = 20.
Answer: ≈ 20
Watch outRound the numbers so they divide EASILY. 600 ÷ 30 is much simpler than 621 ÷ 29.

Worked example 7 - real-world total

You buy 3 items at £6.75 each. Estimate the total.

  1. Read it. Estimate the total of 3 items at £6.75 each.
  2. The fact. Round the price. Keep small counts as they are.
  3. £6.75 → £7.
  4. 3 × £7 = £21.
Answer: ≈ £21
Watch outSmall numbers like 3 don't need rounding. They're already easy.

Worked example 8 - is it enough

You have £20. Is that enough for 3 items at £6.75 each?

  1. Read it. Estimate the cost, then compare with what you have.
  2. The fact. Estimate first. Then compare.
  3. Estimate cost: 3 × £7 = £21.
  4. You have £20.
  5. £20 < £21.
Answer: NO — not enough
Watch outThe exact cost is 3 × £6.75 = £20.25. Even the exact answer confirms £20 is not enough.
