Money

Read £ and p. Convert between them. Add, subtract, and work out change.

The six facts you need

Every money question uses one of these. You don't need to remember anything else.

1 · The formatMoney is written £X.YY. The two digits after the decimal are pence.
2 · £1 = 100pTo go from pounds to pence: × 100. To go from pence to pounds: ÷ 100.
3 · Zero holds a place£3.05 has 5p on top of £3 — not 50p. The 0 is a placeholder.
4 · Adding moneyLine up the decimal points. Add pence — if over 100, carry a pound. Add pounds.
5 · Subtracting moneyLine up decimals. If pence is smaller, borrow £1 (100p) from the pounds column.
6 · ChangeChange = amount paid − cost.

Worked example 1 - convert £ to p

Convert £2.50 to pence.

  1. Read it. Change £2.50 into pence.
  2. The fact. £1 = 100p. Multiply pounds by 100 to get pence.
  3. £2.50 × 100 = 250.
  4. So £2.50 = 250p.
Answer: 250p
Watch outDo not forget the pence part. £2.50 is NOT just 200p.

Worked example 2 - convert p to £

Convert 175p to pounds.

  1. Read it. Change 175p into pounds.
  2. The fact. £1 = 100p. Divide pence by 100 to get pounds.
  3. 175 ÷ 100 = 1.75.
  4. So 175p = £1.75.
Answer: £1.75
Watch outThe digits go the SAME WAY — just add the decimal point 2 places from the right.

Worked example 3 - zero as a placeholder

In £3.05, how many extra pence are there on top of the £3?

  1. Read it. Find the pence part of £3.05.
  2. The fact. The digit AFTER the decimal is TENS OF PENCE. The digit after that is PENCE.
  3. £3.05 = 3 pounds + 05 pence.
  4. The 0 is in the tens-of-pence spot (means no tens).
  5. The 5 is in the pence spot.
Answer: 5p
Watch outA common mistake: reading £3.05 as ‘three pounds fifty’. It is really ‘three pounds five’.

Worked example 4 - add money

£2.40 + £1.30 = ?

  1. Read it. Add £2.40 and £1.30.
  2. The fact. Line up the decimal points and add like normal decimals.
  3. Pence: 40 + 30 = 70.
  4. Pounds: 2 + 1 = 3.
  5. So total = £3.70.
Answer: £3.70
Watch outKeep the decimal point in the SAME COLUMN in your answer as in the numbers you added.

Worked example 5 - adding with carry

£1.65 + £2.50 = ?

  1. Read it. Add £1.65 and £2.50.
  2. The fact. When pence add up to more than 100, carry to the pounds column.
  3. Pence: 65 + 50 = 115. That's 1 pound and 15p.
  4. Pounds: 1 + 2 + 1 (carried) = 4.
  5. Pence: 15.
  6. So total = £4.15.
Answer: £4.15
Watch outWhen pence adds up to more than 100, do NOT write ‘115p’. Carry the 100 as £1.

Worked example 6 - subtract money

£5.00 − £1.75 = ?

  1. Read it. Take £1.75 away from £5.00.
  2. The fact. Line up decimals. If pence is smaller, borrow £1 (100p) from pounds.
  3. Pence: 00 − 75. Can't. Borrow £1 (100p) from pounds.
  4. Now: 100 − 75 = 25p.
  5. Pounds: (5 − 1) − 1 = 3.
  6. So £5.00 − £1.75 = £3.25.
Answer: £3.25
Watch outWhen you borrow from pounds, that £1 becomes 100p in the pence column.

Worked example 7 - change from £10

You pay with £10 for something costing £6.85. What is your change?

  1. Read it. Find change from £10 for £6.85.
  2. The fact. Change = paid − cost.
  3. £10.00 − £6.85.
  4. Pence: 00 − 85. Borrow £1: 100 − 85 = 15.
  5. Pounds: (10 − 6) − 1 = 3.
  6. So change = £3.15.
Answer: £3.15
Watch outChange from £10 is NOT £10 minus the pounds only — subtract the pence too.

Worked example 8 - multi-step change

You buy 3 pens at £2.50 each and pay with £10. What is your change?

  1. Read it. Find change from £10 for 3 pens at £2.50 each.
  2. The fact. First, total cost = each × how many. Then, change = paid − total.
  3. Total cost: 3 × £2.50 = £7.50.
  4. Change: £10.00 − £7.50 = £2.50.
Answer: £2.50
Watch outWork out the total FIRST, then subtract. Do not subtract the price of one item and forget the rest.
