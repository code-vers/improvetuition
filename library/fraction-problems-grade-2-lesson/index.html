Fraction Problems

Multi-step questions — choose the right method, then read the answer in context.

12 questions · 3 sections · four interactive worked examples · no time pressure

What's really happening

These problems don't test a new skill — they test whether you can pick the right tool (multiply, add, compare) and then read the answer in context. The arithmetic itself is the simplifying, multiplying and dividing from the Fractions and Multiplying & Dividing Fractions pages.

Two habits that win marks: when an answer counts whole items (packs, buses, tins), a fraction means you must round up. And when a question says “the rest”, that fraction is 1 minus the others.

Type 4 · What fraction is left?

Read it in contextThe arithmetic answer isn't always the final answer. 3⅗ packs of butter means buying 4; “how many are left” needs 1 minus the rest; and “closer to 1” compares distances, so the bigger fraction can still be the closer one.
Learning Objective 1 · Year 6–7
Section A · Multiply & interpret
Multiply a fraction in a real context, then read the answer — rounding to whole items where needed.
Success criteria — I can:
  • multiply a fraction by a whole number
  • convert to a mixed number to interpret it
  • round up when only whole items can be bought
Learning Objective 2 · Year 7–8
Section B · What fraction is left?
Combine fractions and find the remaining fraction using “1 minus the rest”.
Success criteria — I can:
  • add fractions with a common denominator
  • subtract from 1 to find “the rest”
  • give the answer in its simplest form
Learning Objective 3 · Year 8–9
Section C · Fractions of fractions & comparing
Take a fraction of a fraction, and compare fractions by their distance from a value.
Success criteria — I can:
  • find a fraction of a fraction by multiplying
  • add the parts over a common denominator
  • compare distances from 1
